THE THINKING BOX.

What if a simple little box could get students to think a little differently? A physical classroom tool designed to encourage independent thinking, different perspectives, and human connection as AI becomes part of education.

01
Starting with the big question

Why are we thinking about this?

AI is becoming more common in education, and there are a lot of opportunities for how it can support students and teachers. But there are also concerns about students relying too much on AI instead of developing their own ideas, problem-solving skills, and ability to communicate with others.

Our project looks at how AI can be part of education without taking away the parts of learning that come from thinking for yourself, hearing different perspectives, and interacting with other people.

How might we integrate AI into education while preserving students' cognitive development, social skills, and human connection?

03
Meet the intervention

A little box with a bigger purpose.

The Thinking Box is a physical classroom tool that uses perspective coins to help students expand their thinking. Instead of immediately looking for an answer, students select a coin with a prompt that encourages them to consider an idea from another point of view.

The goal is to make students slow down, question their first interpretation, and build on ideas through discussion with their classmates. The box is meant to support learning, not replace the student, teacher, or conversation happening in the classroom.

What could this look like in a classroom?

Imagine a class reading The Great Gatsby. The teacher asks, "Why does Gatsby go to such great lengths to be with Daisy?" A student draws a coin, uses its prompt to think about the question, and discusses their idea with classmates. The coin can then be flipped and passed to another student, who continues the discussion using that same perspective.

05
Co-design workshop

Getting other people involved.

We planned a co-design workshop to get input from participants and explore how AI could be available in education while still supporting students' social skills, cognitive development, and human connection.

Instead of only presenting our idea, we wanted participants to brainstorm their own classroom experiences and then use the perspective coins to challenge or expand their initial ideas. This would help us understand how the Thinking Box could be used and what might need to change.

ADD WORKSHOP SETUP / MATERIALS PHOTO

Our practice round

Before facilitating the actual co-design workshop, we completed a practice round to go through the activities and see what parts of the process needed to be clearer or adjusted.

We wanted to make the Thinking Box easier to understand, so we planned step-by-step slides explaining how it works in a classroom and how participants would use it during the workshop.

Sticky notes were not enough for developing and communicating ideas, so we planned to include larger sheets of paper for participants to sketch their concepts.

We decided to separate the reflection activity from the Thinking Box. A four-section matrix would be used to reflect on the impact of the coin instead. This keeps the box focused on selecting perspective coins.

We planned to make the introduction and closing more structured, so participants understand the purpose of the workshop, what they will be doing, and how their ideas will help us continue developing the intervention.

My individual iteration
Taking the physical product further ✳

Making the box work better.

For my individual iteration, I want to focus on the Thinking Box itself and how the physical product works. Based on the practice round, I started thinking more about how students interact with the box and how its design can make the experience easier to understand and use.

One thing we noticed during the practice round was that using the box for both selecting coins and reflecting on the activity made the process confusing. We decided to move the reflection activity to a separate four-section matrix, allowing the box to focus on the perspective coins.

I want to build on this by exploring how the physical design could better guide students through the activity. This includes looking at how the coins are organized, how students select and return them, and how the box makes it clear what they should do next.

A little reflection

Keeping the human part of learning.

This project has helped me think about how a physical intervention can encourage students to approach learning differently, especially as AI becomes more common in education. The Thinking Box is not meant to keep AI out of the classroom, but to make sure students still have opportunities to think for themselves and learn from other people.

The next step is to use the co-design workshop to learn more about how the intervention could work in practice. I also plan to continue developing the physical box so that the way students interact with it is clear and easy to follow.
