01 / Project Background

Why this project?

As AI becomes more common in education, there are new opportunities for how students learn and how teachers support them. At the same time, there are concerns about students relying too much on AI instead of developing their own ideas, problem-solving skills, and ability to communicate with others.

Our project focuses on exploring how AI can be part of education without taking away the parts of learning that come from thinking for yourself, hearing different perspectives, and interacting with other people.

How might we integrate AI into education while preserving students' cognitive development, social skills, and human connection?
02 / Futures Thinking

The future we want to move toward

Through our futures work, we explored different ways AI could change education. We focused on a preferred future where AI is available as a learning support, but students still have opportunities to think independently, develop their own opinions, and learn from one another.

01 / CURRENT CONCERNS

AI replacing parts of learning

Students may become more dependent on AI for answers and ideas, with fewer opportunities to work through problems, communicate, and develop their own thinking.

02 / PREFERRED FUTURE

AI supporting learning

AI is used as a tool, while students continue to develop independent thinking, explore different viewpoints, and build connections with classmates and teachers.

03 / The Intervention

Introducing the Thinking Box

The Thinking Box is a physical classroom tool that uses perspective coins to help students expand their thinking. Instead of immediately looking for an answer, students select a coin with a prompt that encourages them to consider an idea from another point of view.

The goal is to make students slow down, question their first interpretation, and build on ideas through discussion with their classmates. The box is meant to support learning, not replace the teacher, the student, or the conversation happening in the classroom.

06 / Co-design Approach

Learning with participants

We planned a co-design workshop to get input from participants and explore how AI could be available in education while still supporting students' social skills, cognitive development, and human connection.

Instead of only presenting our idea, we wanted participants to brainstorm their own classroom experiences and then use the perspective coins to challenge or expand their initial ideas. This would help us understand how the Thinking Box could be used and what might need to change.

07 / Practice Round

Preparing for the workshop

Before facilitating the actual co-design workshop, we completed a practice round to go through the activities and see what parts of the process needed to be clearer or adjusted.

The practice round helped us identify areas where the instructions, materials, and overall flow could be improved before working with our actual participants.

Clearer instructions

We wanted to make the Thinking Box easier to understand, so we planned step-by-step slides explaining how it works in a classroom and how participants would use it during the workshop.

More space to sketch

Sticky notes were not enough for developing and communicating ideas, so we planned to include larger sheets of paper for participants to sketch their concepts.

Clearer reflection

We decided to separate the reflection activity from the Thinking Box. A four-section matrix would be used to reflect on the impact of the coin instead.

Changes planned for the actual facilitation

We also planned to make the introduction and closing more structured, so participants understand the purpose of the workshop, what they will be doing, and how their ideas will help us continue developing the intervention.

08 / Updated Workshop Plan

40-minute co-design workshop

The updated workshop is designed to move participants from thinking about AI in education to developing and expanding their own ideas using the Thinking Box.

Activity Time What participants will do Facilitator
Introduction 5 min Introduce the two future states, workshop goals, and participant roles. Ask participants to imagine a future where AI supports education while independent thinking and human connection remain important. Laura
Warm-up 5 min Complete a Rose, Bud, Thorn activity. Participants identify what is useful about AI in education, where it might hinder learning, and what new possibilities could support the preferred future. Laura
Intervention presentation 5 min Introduce the Thinking Box and explain how the perspective coins work in a classroom and during the workshop. Fatima

Materials

Participants

Anyone interested in education and AI, with a mix of educators and students being ideal.

09 / Individual Iteration

Continuing to develop the Thinking Box

For my individual iteration, I want to focus on the Thinking Box itself and how the physical product works. Based on the practice round, I started thinking more about how students interact with the box and how its design can make the experience easier to understand and use.

One thing we noticed during the practice round was that using the box for both selecting coins and reflecting on the activity made the process confusing. We decided to move the reflection activity to a separate four-section matrix, allowing the box to focus on the perspective coins.

My design direction

Making the box easier to use

I want to explore how the physical design of the Thinking Box could better guide students through the activity. This includes looking at how the coins are organized, how students select and return them, and how the box makes it clear what students should do next.

I also want to explore how the four sections of the box could work with the different perspective coins and whether the physical layout makes the interaction more clear for students. The goal is to make the box feel like a simple classroom tool that students can use without needing a lot of explanation.

11 / Reflection

Where the project goes next

This project has helped me think about how a physical intervention can encourage students to approach learning differently, especially as AI becomes more common in education. The Thinking Box is not meant to keep AI out of the classroom, but to make sure students still have opportunities to think for themselves and learn from other people.

The next step is to use the co-design workshop to learn more about how the intervention could work in practice. I also plan to continue developing the physical box so that the way students interact with it is clear and easy to follow.
